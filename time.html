A mask slows the clock

The longer you breathe the same air, the more you take in. A mask's protection factor tells you how much it slows that down: in a 10× mask, every 10 minutes counts as about 1 minute with no mask.

What speeds the clock up

The clock doesn't tick at the same rate everywhere. The same hour can count for much more or much less depending on the air and what you're doing.

Faster

More in the air, or more air breathed

  • Crowded, stuffy rooms, where particles build up
  • Wildfire smoke or dusty work
  • Breathing hard: exercise, singing, talking loudly
  • Being close to the source, such as a sick person or a fire
Slower

Less in the air, or less of it reaching you

  • Fresh air: open windows, outdoors, good ventilation
  • Air filters and purifiers in the room
  • Distance from the source
  • A higher protection factor, and a mask that seals well

That is why a better mask matters most where the clock runs fastest: long, crowded, stuffy or smoky situations.

Where this picture is too simple

  • Amount breathed in isn't the same as risk. Whether you get ill depends on how much of a virus is in the air, your immunity and more. Breathing in less lowers the odds, but twice the amount doesn't mean exactly twice the risk.
  • The rating assumes a good fit. A mask with gaps at the nose or cheeks lets in much more than its rating, so its clock runs faster too.
  • Real ratings vary. Cloth, face mask and ear-loop figures are rough estimates from studies. Respirator ratings are official workplace minimums, and a well-fitted one often does better.

Protection factors are UK HSE and US OSHA assigned ratings, the same ones the mask picker uses; cloth, face mask and ear-loop figures are rough estimates.
